9. Platform Overview

The Live Quiz Platform is best understood through the lifecycle of a typical session, from creation through to reporting.

An administrator or quiz creator begins by building a quiz manually, or by using the platform's AI-assisted generation to produce a complete quiz from a topic, an uploaded PDF or Word document, a URL, or pasted text with every AI-generated question reviewed and editable before use. The quiz is then branded with the organisation's own logo, colours, and theme, and can be organised into a reusable question bank tagged by subject and difficulty for future sessions.

When it is time to run a session, the host launches the quiz live. Participants join in seconds using a QR code, a direct link, or a session PIN, without needing to create an account. As the session runs, the platform is server-authoritative for timing and scoring: the server determines when a question opens and closes and calculates results, while participant and host devices render what is happening in real time including live answer submission, a live leaderboard, and automatic reconnection if a participant's network connection drops mid-session. At the close of the session, the top three participants are shown on a podium, alongside the full participant ranking.

After the session ends, results are not lost. They are captured in the platform's reporting and session history, giving administrators and management access to participation data, performance analysis, and historical comparisons across sessions described in full in Section 14.

16. Real-Time Architecture

Live sessions depend on the platform's real-time layer behaving predictably under load, and behaving fairly for every participant regardless of network conditions. This section describes how that is achieved.

16.1 Session Flow

A live session follows a consistent, server-driven flow from join through to the final leaderboard.

StepWhat happens
1. JoinParticipants connect over a WebSocket session as they scan a QR code, follow a direct link, or enter a session PIN; the host's lobby view updates in real time as each participant joins
2. Question broadcastWhen the host opens a question, the server broadcasts the question to all connected participants at the same moment
3. Answer submissionParticipants submit answers over the WebSocket connection while the question is live; the correct answer is never sent to a participant's device before the question closes
4. Server-side scoringThe server, not the client, determines when the question closes, evaluates each submitted answer, and calculates the resulting score
5. Leaderboard pushUpdated rankings are pushed to the host and participant devices in real time once scoring is complete

16.2 Server-Authoritative Timing, Reconnection, and Why It Matters at Scale

Question timing, closure, and scoring are controlled by the server, not by any individual device: participant and host devices render a countdown, an open or closed question, and an updated leaderboard, but they never decide when a question opens or closes and are never trusted with the correct answer before it is safe to reveal it. Where a participant's device loses connectivity mid-session common in venues with variable Wi-Fi or mobile coverage the platform automatically attempts to reconnect the session rather than dropping the participant. For a session of the scale described in Section 22, this combination is what keeps a large, live audience in a fair, synchronised session rather than a collection of individually drifting client experiences.
